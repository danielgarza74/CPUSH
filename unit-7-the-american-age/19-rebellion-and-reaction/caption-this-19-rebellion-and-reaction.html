How this works (15–20 minutes)

  1. Look at each of the 4 images. Read the “What you’re looking at” line.
  2. Write a short, funny caption, like a meme. Pick a caption style if you want one.
  3. Then write 2–3 sentences about what is really going on. Use at least one key term from the lesson. Type the key term in English, exactly as shown.
  4. Save Progress any time. When all 4 are done, press Finished and upload the file to Canvas.

Keep it respectful: make the joke about the company, the idea, or the situation. Never joke about workers, children, immigrants, enslaved or freed people, Native peoples, anyone’s race or religion, or people who were hurt.

The sky is black, and the ground is covered with footprints. Your textbook has a part called “A Unifying Moment: ‘The Eagle Has Landed.’” This photo goes with that moment, the Apollo 11 landing in July 1969.

Heads up: This is a proud moment in U.S. history. Aim your joke at the flag, the footprints, the empty gray ground, or the black sky. Never joke about the astronaut as a person or about anyone who worked on the mission.

  • Look at the flag and the footprints. What do they tell you about who was here and what they wanted to show?
  • Think about 1969 at home: protests, the war, and the Stonewall Uprising. Why might a moon landing feel like “a unifying moment”?
