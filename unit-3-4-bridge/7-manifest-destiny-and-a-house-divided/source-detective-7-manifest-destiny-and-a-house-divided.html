1846

Case file 1 of 5

Who: David Wilmot, a U.S. congressman from Pennsylvania · From: An amendment to a bill in Congress (the Wilmot Proviso)

Wilmot offered this during the war with Mexico, before the United States had won any new land.

“. . . as an express and fundamental condition to the acquisition of any territory from the Republic of Mexico . . . neither slavery nor involuntary servitude shall ever exist in any part of said territory, except for crime, whereof the party shall first be duly convicted.”
English original: “. . . as an express and fundamental condition to the acquisition of any territory from the Republic of Mexico . . . neither slavery nor involuntary servitude shall ever exist in any part of said territory, except for crime, whereof the party shall first be duly convicted.”

David Wilmot, Amendment to H.R. 534 (Wilmot Proviso), August 8, 1846, Congressional Globe, 29th Cong., 1st Sess., 1217.

Word help: proviso = a condition added to a law · involuntary servitude = forced labor (slavery) · convicted = found guilty

Hint: What does Wilmot say can never exist in the new land?

1850

Case file 2 of 5

Who: Jermain Loguen, a Black minister who had escaped slavery · From: A speech at a public meeting in Syracuse, New York

Loguen spoke a few weeks after Congress passed the Fugitive Slave Act of 1850.

“I don’t respect this law—I don’t fear it—I won’t obey it! It outlaws me, and I outlaw it, and the men who attempt to enforce it on me.”
English original: “I don’t respect this law—I don’t fear it—I won’t obey it! It outlaws me, and I outlaw it, and the men who attempt to enforce it on me.”

Jermain Wesley Loguen, speech, Syracuse, N.Y., October 4, 1850, in The Rev. J. W. Loguen, as a Slave and as a Freeman (Syracuse: J. G. K. Truair, 1859), 391–94.

Word help: outlaws = puts someone outside the protection of the law · enforce = make people follow a law

Hint: What does Loguen say he will do about the law?

1854

Case file 3 of 5

Who: The U.S. Congress · From: A law (the Kansas-Nebraska Act)

This law said who would decide about slavery in two new territories.

“. . . it being the true intent and meaning of this act not to legislate slavery into any Territory or State, nor to exclude it therefrom, but to leave the people thereof perfectly free to form and regulate their domestic institutions in their own way, subject only to the Constitution of the United States.”
English original: “. . . it being the true intent and meaning of this act not to legislate slavery into any Territory or State, nor to exclude it therefrom, but to leave the people thereof perfectly free to form and regulate their domestic institutions in their own way, subject only to the Constitution of the United States.”

An Act to Organize the Territories of Nebraska and Kansas, May 30, 1854, sec. 14, Statutes at Large 10:277.

Word help: legislate = make laws about · exclude = keep out · domestic institutions = a community’s own ways of living and working (here, slavery)

Hint: Who does the law say will decide about slavery: Congress or the people of the territory?

1860

Case file 4 of 5

Who: South Carolina (a state convention) · From: A declaration of the causes of secession

South Carolina explained why it was leaving the Union after Lincoln was elected.

“. . . a sectional party has found within that Article establishing the Executive Department, the means of subverting the Constitution itself. A geographical line has been drawn across the Union, and all the States north of that line have united in the election of a man to the high office of President of the United States, whose opinions and purposes are hostile to slavery. . . . [T]he Union heretofore existing between this State and the other States of North America, is dissolved.”
English original: “. . . a sectional party has found within that Article establishing the Executive Department, the means of subverting the Constitution itself. A geographical line has been drawn across the Union, and all the States north of that line have united in the election of a man to the high office of President of the United States, whose opinions and purposes are hostile to slavery. . . . [T]he Union heretofore existing between this State and the other States of North America, is dissolved.”

South Carolina Convention, “Declaration of the Immediate Causes Which Induce and Justify the Secession of South Carolina from the Federal Union,” December 24, 1860.

Word help: sectional = favoring one region · subverting = destroying · hostile = against

Hint: Which result of the election does South Carolina blame, and what does it do next?

1861

Case file 5 of 5

Who: President Abraham Lincoln · From: An inaugural address (his first speech as President)

Lincoln answered the seceding states in his first speech as President.

“I hold that in contemplation of universal law and of the Constitution the Union of these States is perpetual. . . . [N]o State upon its own mere motion can lawfully get out of the Union; that resolves and ordinances to that effect are legally void.”
English original: “I hold that in contemplation of universal law and of the Constitution the Union of these States is perpetual. . . . [N]o State upon its own mere motion can lawfully get out of the Union; that resolves and ordinances to that effect are legally void.”

Abraham Lincoln, First Inaugural Address, March 4, 1861.

Word help: perpetual = lasting forever · lawfully = legally · ordinances = laws passed by a state convention · void = having no force

Hint: Can a state leave the Union on its own, according to Lincoln?
