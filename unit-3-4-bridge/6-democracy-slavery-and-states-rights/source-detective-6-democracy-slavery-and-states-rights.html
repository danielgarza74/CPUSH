1820

Case file 1 of 5

Who: The U.S. Congress · From: A law (the Missouri Compromise)

Congress passed this law in 1820 to settle the fight over Missouri.

“In all that territory ceded by France to the United States, under the name of Louisiana, which lies north of thirty-six degrees and thirty minutes north latitude, . . . slavery and involuntary servitude . . . shall be, and is hereby, forever prohibited.”
English original: “In all that territory ceded by France to the United States, under the name of Louisiana, which lies north of thirty-six degrees and thirty minutes north latitude, . . . slavery and involuntary servitude . . . shall be, and is hereby, forever prohibited.”

“An Act to authorize the people of the Missouri territory to form a constitution and state government . . . and to prohibit slavery in certain territories,” March 6, 1820, Section 8.

Word help: ceded = given up · prohibited = not allowed · involuntary servitude = forced labor (slavery)

Hint: Who is making this rule, and for how much land?

1831

Case file 2 of 5

Who: William Lloyd Garrison, a White abolitionist · From: A newspaper editorial (The Liberator)

Garrison started a newspaper in Boston to demand an end to slavery.

“I am in earnest — I will not equivocate — I will not excuse — I will not retreat a single inch — AND I WILL BE HEARD.”
English original: “I am in earnest — I will not equivocate — I will not excuse — I will not retreat a single inch — AND I WILL BE HEARD.”

William Lloyd Garrison, “To the Public,” The Liberator, Boston, January 1, 1831.

Word help: equivocate = speak in a way that is not clear · excuse = give a reason to avoid blame · retreat = go back

Hint: What does Garrison refuse to do, and what does he demand?

1832

Case file 3 of 5

Who: South Carolina (a state convention) · From: A state law (the Ordinance of Nullification)

A South Carolina convention passed this after the tariffs of 1828 and 1832.

“[The tariff acts of 1828 and 1832] . . . are null, void, and no law, nor binding upon this State, its officers or citizens.”
English original: “[The tariff acts of 1828 and 1832] . . . are null, void, and no law, nor binding upon this State, its officers or citizens.”

South Carolina, Ordinance of Nullification, November 24, 1832.

Word help: null = canceled · void = having no force · binding = forcing someone to obey

Hint: What does the state call the tariff laws?

1832

Case file 4 of 5

Who: President Andrew Jackson · From: A public statement (the Proclamation to South Carolina)

President Jackson answered South Carolina with this public statement.

“I consider, then, the power to annul a law of the United States, assumed by one State, incompatible with the existence of the Union, contradicted expressly by the letter of the Constitution, unauthorized by its spirit, inconsistent with every principle on which it was founded, and destructive of the great object for which it was formed.” . . . “[D]isunion, by armed force, is TREASON.”
English original: “I consider, then, the power to annul a law of the United States, assumed by one State, incompatible with the existence of the Union, contradicted expressly by the letter of the Constitution, unauthorized by its spirit, inconsistent with every principle on which it was founded, and destructive of the great object for which it was formed.” . . . “[D]isunion, by armed force, is TREASON.”

Andrew Jackson, Proclamation Regarding Nullification, December 10, 1832.

Word help: annul = cancel · incompatible = cannot exist together · disunion = breaking up the Union · treason = betraying your country

Hint: Whose power does Jackson say a state cannot cancel?

1836

Case file 5 of 5

Who: Chief John Ross, leader of the Cherokee Nation · From: A letter to Congress

Ross is writing about the Treaty of New Echota (1835), which was signed by a small group of Cherokee men.

“The instrument in question is not the act of our Nation; we are not parties to its covenants; it has not received the sanction of our people.”
English original: “The instrument in question is not the act of our Nation; we are not parties to its covenants; it has not received the sanction of our people.”

John Ross, Letter “To the Senate and House of Representatives,” Red Clay Council Ground, Cherokee Nation, September 28, 1836.

Word help: instrument = a formal document · covenants = promises in a treaty · sanction = approval

Hint: Does Ross say the Cherokee people agreed to the treaty?
