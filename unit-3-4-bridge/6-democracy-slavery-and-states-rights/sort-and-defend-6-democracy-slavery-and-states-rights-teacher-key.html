SORT & DEFEND 6

Who Has the Final Say?

Democracy, Slavery, and States’ Rights · Sort the Power

BRIDGE QUESTION: Who has the final say: the national government or the states?
Teacher tally on the key: NATIONAL 5 · STATE 4. Cards 3 and 6 are the debatable ones: accept either side with sound reasoning from the readings. Students need only a short phrase for each reason.

Part 1 · Sort the Power

1820 · Reading A

Card 1 · The Missouri Compromise

Maine joined as a free state and Missouri as a slave state. Congress also banned slavery in the rest of the Louisiana Purchase north of latitude 36°30′.

Who decided where slavery could spread? Name who did it.

NATIONAL Congress made the deal and drew the 36°30′ line. That is national power.
1821 · Reading B

Card 2 · New York Limits Black Voting

New York’s new constitution gave the vote to all White men. At the same time it added new limits on Black voting.

Who decided who could vote? Name who did it.

STATE The New York state government decided. Voting rules were a state choice here.
1832–33 · Reading C

Card 3 · South Carolina Says “No” to a Tariff

South Carolina called the tariffs “null, void, and no law.” Jackson sent soldiers and a warship to Charleston. In March 1833 Congress passed a compromise tariff and the Force Bill. Both sides claimed victory.

Both sides claimed victory. Who really had the final say? Explain.

NATIONAL National. A state could not cancel a federal law. Jackson “upheld the supremacy of the Union.” (South Carolina did get a lower tariff, so accept a STATE answer only with strong reasoning.)
1830 · Reading D

Card 4 · The Indian Removal Act

Congress let Jackson ignore earlier treaty promises. The law forced nearly 100,000 Native people in the East and South to move west of the Mississippi River.

Who made this decision? Name who did it.

NATIONAL Congress passed the law and Jackson carried it out. That is national power used against Native nations.
1830 · Reading D

Card 5 · Georgia Takes Over Cherokee Rights

Georgia announced that state law now covered the Cherokee. Cherokee people could not vote, own property, or testify against Whites in court.

Who made this decision? Name who did it.

STATE The Georgia legislature. That is state power used against the Cherokee.
1832 · Reading D

Card 6 · Worcester v. Georgia

The Supreme Court said Georgia’s laws broke the Constitution and U.S. treaties. Chief Justice Marshall said the Cherokee Nation was “a distinct political community” where Georgia law had no force. Jackson, however, forced the Cherokee to obey Georgia’s laws or move west.

The Court ruled for the Cherokee, but they were still forced out. Who really had the final say, and why?

NATIONAL National, but in a twist: President Jackson chose to back Georgia, so the national government’s own leader decided the outcome. Accept STATE if the student explains that Georgia got its way.
1838 · Reading D

Card 7 · The Trail of Tears

While Martin Van Buren was president, 17,000 Cherokee were forced west under military guard. Thousands died from cold, disease, or starvation.

Who carried out this removal? Name who did it.

NATIONAL The president and the U.S. military. That is national power.
1800s · Reading E

Card 8 · Slave Codes

Each state made its own slave codes. Enslaved people needed a pass to leave, had nightly curfews, and in some places it was a crime for them to learn to read and write.

Who wrote and enforced these rules? Name who did it.

STATE State governments. Each state wrote its own slave code.
1831 · Reading F

Card 9 · After Nat Turner’s Rebellion

Virginia’s legislature debated ending slavery and voted it down. Instead it limited reading, writing, and religious meetings. Southern states also set up White patrols to look for freedom seekers.

Who decided how to respond to the rebellion? Name who did it.

STATE Virginia’s legislature and other southern states. That is state power.

Part 2 · Who Had No Say?

Look back at your 9 cards. Some groups never got to decide anything. Name two groups of people who had no say, and tell how you know.

Any two: enslaved people (slave codes, the domestic slave trade); the Cherokee and other Native nations (Removal Act, Georgia laws); free Black men in New York (1821 limits); women. Douglass: “What have I, or those I represent, to do with your national independence?”

Part 3 · Final Round: CER (sample answer)

From 1819 to 1850, the national government had the final say more often. (A student may claim STATES and rebut with the Force Bill; score the reasoning.)
In the Missouri Compromise (1820), Congress decided where slavery could spread by drawing the 36°30′ line.
In the Nullification Crisis, South Carolina declared the tariffs “null, void, and no law,” but Congress passed the Force Bill (1833) and Jackson “upheld the supremacy of the Union.”
Both times a big question about states’ rights came up, the national government made the decision, and the states had to accept it.
Someone could say the states had the final say, because Georgia took Cherokee land even after the Supreme Court ruled against it in Worcester v. Georgia (1832), and southern states made their own slave codes. But Georgia won only because President Jackson, a national leader, chose to back it.
