How this works (15–20 minutes)

  1. Look at each of the 4 images. Read the “What you’re looking at” line.
  2. Write a short, funny caption, like a meme. Pick a caption style if you want one.
  3. Then write 2–3 sentences about what is really going on. Use at least one key term from the lesson. Type the key term in English, exactly as shown.
  4. Save Progress any time. When all 4 are done, press Finished and upload the file to Canvas.

Keep it respectful: make the joke about the company, the idea, or the situation. Never joke about workers, children, immigrants, enslaved or freed people, Native peoples, anyone’s race or religion, or people who were hurt.

The “Laocoön” cartoon (1938)

Not done

What you’re looking at: A black-and-white cartoon from Norton Ch. 24. A tall man in a dark coat and striped pants, who stands for the United States, is wrapped up in a giant snake. The snake is labeled ISOLATION. He stands on a stone base marked LAOCOÖN 1938. A man on the left points and says, “HELP THEM!” A man on the right holds an olive branch and says, “DO NOTHING!” Norton says the 1938 cartoon shows U.S. foreign policy tangled up in the serpent of isolationism.

Heads up: This is a political cartoon about a big argument over war. Aim your joke at the snake, the shouting, or the stone base. Never joke about people who were hurt in the wars.

  • Read the two speech bubbles. What does each man want the United States to do?
  • Look at the snake. Why is the main figure stuck?

A man in a long light coat and an officer’s cap stands at the left and salutes. A training plane sits at each side of the line. Norton says these are the first graduates of the Tuskegee Airmen, the first African American military pilots, being reviewed at Tuskegee, Alabama, in 1941.

Heads up: This is a serious one. Norton says African Americans in the military faced prejudice. Write one clear line. No jokes about the men, their race, or how they stand. Look at the line, the planes, and the jackets.

  • Look at the line of men. What do their clothes and the way they stand tell you?
  • Look at the planes. What were these men trained to do?
